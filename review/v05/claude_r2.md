OVERALL: 7/10

CHARACTER: 7/10 - Bigger backpack and hair read well at a glance, but the face is still a soft smudge: eyes, nose and mouth are crammed low on the head-sphere with no separation, so it doesn't yet have the charm of the pets/props.
SCOOTER: 7/10 - Basket and mirrors fixes landed well (clearly rounded wicker drum, tapered mirrors). Front fairing is still one undifferentiated pink blob — headlight, fender and body shield aren't read as separate forms.
PETS: 8/10 - Cat's smaller face patch, rounded ears and tapered tail are a clear win and it now reads distinct from the dog. Eyes are still flat oversized black discs, slightly generic/plush.
FACADES: 6/10 - Clean rounded windows and legible signage, but house and shop use the *same* stacked-ring/tire motif, so they don't read as different buildings. Shop's roof is a flat grey hemisphere that looks like sheet metal, clashing with the pastel toy palette.
STREET: 8/10 - Wider gutter and raised manholes read nicely and add believable scale; lamp posts and tree shapes are solid. Distant buildings repeat the same ring motif from FACADES, so the monotony issue shows up here too.
INTERIOR: 7/10 - TV and clock redesigns are genuinely charming toy-shapes. Everything else (wardrobe, nightstand, bookshelf) is still plain rectangular prisms that don't match the new rounded language, and the coffee table's thin legs look flimsy under its big round top.

TOP FIXES:
1. Give house and shop distinct facade motifs — the stacked circular ring/tire relief is reused on both buildings (and repeats down the street), so nothing reads as a unique landmark. Swap one set for a different primitive (e.g. flower rosette of small tori, or stacked jar cylinders).
2. Repaint/reshape the shop roof — it's a flat grey hemisphere that reads as industrial sheet metal; recolor pastel and add a few small domed skylight bumps to match the toy language.
3. Break the scooter's front fairing into separate rounded forms — right now headlight, fender and shield fuse into one blob; model each as its own sphere/torus/rounded-box with a visible seam/bevel between them.
4. Rework the rider's face layout — raise and separate the eyes (two ovals with highlight spheres) from the nose-mouth cluster so they don't crowd together into a smudge at the chin.
5. Add rounded-corner bevels and small torus/dome pulls to interior furniture (wardrobe, nightstand, bookshelf) so they match the new TV/clock shape language instead of staying plain rectangular prisms.
6. Thicken the coffee table's legs into tapered cones or a single rounded pedestal cylinder — the current thin legs look structurally flimsy under the oversized round top.
7. Shrink the living-room rug ~25-30% and add a torus-outline border so it reads as a placed object rather than flattening the whole floor.
8. Reduce cat eye diameter slightly and add a colored iris ring plus offset highlight sphere — currently flat black discs that look generic/doll-like compared to the rest of the redesign.
