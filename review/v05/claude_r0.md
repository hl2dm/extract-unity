I have enough visual detail from the full-resolution screenshots already reviewed. Here's the assessment:

OVERALL: 5/10

CHARACTER: 4/10 - Kid-on-scooter reads as a flat silhouette (round yellow head, cap, blue jacket) with almost no secondary shapes — no visible hands/gloves, backpack, shoe volumes, or face features at a readable distance; body is mostly occluded by the oversized scooter seat/mirror, so the "redesigned toy" silhouette never gets to register.

SCOOTER: 6/10 - The "little sheep" scooter has a nicely rounded, toy-like lathed body (bulbous seat, puffy fenders, round headlight/mirror) that matches the target style well; let down by the mirror housing being an awkward flat disc stuck on a thin cylinder rather than a sculpted toy shape, and the handlebar/stem geometry is thin and realistic compared to the chunky body.

PETS: 3/10 - Cat and dog are the biggest style outlier in the whole set: flat single-tone tan material with almost no shading/gradient, thin spindly stick legs, sharp realistic muzzles/ears, and pointed claws — reads as a low-poly placeholder asset, not a rounded storybook toy animal. Needs a full shape pass to match the rest of the world.

FACADES: 7/10 - g_house and g_shop have good toy-architecture language: rounded piping/tube trim, lathed roof caps, porthole windows, soft bevels, and the "阿姨家"/"柑仔店" signage is clean and legible with good contrast. Window mullions are a bit thin/flat relative to the chunky trim, and the shop reads muddy/low-contrast in the dusk lighting, flattening its toy-bevel detail.

STREET: 7/10 - Lamp posts (rounded cylinder + sphere cap), topiary balls, and shopfront rhythm read as cohesive toy-town; only knock is that mid-ground buildings lose silhouette distinction (repeating pastel boxes) and the road itself is a flat untextured plane with only paint-stripe geometry, no curb/gutter bevel.

INTERIOR: 6/10 - Living room sofa, round coffee table, bookshelf with rounded knick-knacks, and wall clock all nail the soft-toy look; kitchen is weaker — walls are flat slabs with no baseboard/trim bevel, and the doorway arches look like plain cut rectangles rather than the rounded lathed arches used on the exterior doors.

TOP FIXES:
1. Rebuild cat/dog meshes: thicken legs into tapered capsules, round the muzzle/head into soft lathed forms, add gradient/two-tone shading (light belly, darker back) and soft paw pads instead of flat tan + pointed claws — currently the single worst style mismatch.
2. Give the rider kid readable secondary shapes even at distance: bigger round mitten-hands on the grips, a rounded backpack capsule on the back, chunky rounded shoes — right now the silhouette is just a cylinder+sphere blob.
3. Redesign the scooter mirror/headlight assembly as one sculpted rounded-toy unit (torus + sphere) instead of a thin flat disc on a stick, to match the bulbous body language.
4. Add a curb/gutter bevel (rounded rectangular strip) and subtle road texture break between sidewalk and street plane — currently a single flat quad reads cheap next to the detailed buildings.
5. Round the kitchen/interior doorway arches to match the exterior's lathed porthole-and-arch language, and add a baseboard bevel strip where walls meet floor.
6. Re-balance shop facade lighting/palette so the dusk pass doesn't flatten contrast on the rounded trim — boost rim-light or desaturate the purple cast so bevels stay readable.
7. Differentiate mid-street building silhouettes (vary roof cap shapes/heights) so the block doesn't read as repeated pastel boxes in long shots.
8. Thicken window mullions/frames on facades to match the chunky pipe-trim scale elsewhere — currently thin compared to neighboring rounded details.
