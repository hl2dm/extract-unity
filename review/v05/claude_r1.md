OVERALL: 6/10

CHARACTER: 6/10 - Round teddy-bear body/head reads well and the big circular eye-rims are a nice signature, but the head is a plain smooth dome with no hair silhouette (reads bald/egg-shaped), and from behind (g_ride_third) the rider is almost featureless — just a sphere + two shoulder bumps.

SCOOTER: 7/10 - Headlight bezel, round mirrors and chunky tires now sell the toy look well. Two things break it: the rear basket is a hard-edged rectangular crate (realistic cargo-box language, not storybook), and the mirror stalks are thin wires with a ball stuck on top instead of a tapered cone-to-sphere form.

PETS: 7/10 - Big jump from baseline; bodies, paws, and blush cheeks are properly toy-rounded. Still two realism leaks: the cat's ears are sharp realistic triangles (dog's rounded ears look better), and the cat's tail is a beaded/segmented chain instead of a smooth tapered capsule like the dog's puffed tail.

FACADES: 7/10 - g_house is the strongest frame in the set (quatrefoil medallion, round windows, legible sign). g_shop drags the average down — it's rendered in a flat, desaturated purple-grey that breaks continuity with the pastel pink/cream house right next door.

STREET: 7/10 - Composition and lamp/tree shapes are solid, but the new gutter band and manholes are essentially invisible at normal play-camera distance, so the street still reads as one flat grey plane in motion.

INTERIOR: 7/10 - Baseboards and the arched door-corner rosettes are a clear, visible upgrade. The wall-mounted TV is a plain flat rectangle and the clock is a thin-rimmed realistic disc — both read as off-the-shelf real objects next to the otherwise toy-shaped furniture. The magenta ceiling gradient band also looks like a lighting artifact rather than a designed trim.

TOP FIXES:
1. Shop facade: lighten/desaturate the purple-grey panels toward the house's pink/cream palette, add a second light trim color (cream or mint) to break up the monotone mass.
2. Scooter basket: replace the sharp rectangular crate with a rounded-corner hamper/half-capsule bin, matching the bevelled language used elsewhere.
3. Scooter mirrors: taper the stalk from a thick cone at the base down to the sphere instead of a thin uniform rod; shorten stalk length.
4. Interior TV: rebuild as a rounded-corner box with a thick chunky bezel and two small round feet/stand, instead of a flat plain rectangle.
5. Wall clock: thicken the rim into a torus, enlarge painted numerals/dots, shrink the thin realistic hands into stubby rounded wedges.
6. Cat ears: round off the tips (blend toward a tilted hemisphere/rounded triangle) to match the dog's teddy-style ears.
7. Cat tail: swap the beaded/segmented chain for one smooth tapered capsule with a gentle S-curve, consistent with the dog's puffed tail.
8. Street gutter/manholes: widen the gutter stripe and raise the manhole cover into a visibly bevelled disc so the added detail actually reads from the default gameplay camera height.
