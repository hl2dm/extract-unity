Yes — the street/exterior shophouses read as realistic Taiwan architecture with a toon shader and pastel repaint, not a toy diorama. Wall proportions, window rhythm, narrow pillars, and multi-story scale are straight off real buildings; only the roofline blobs and sky say "toy."

**Top 5 issues:**
1. Building bodies keep real-world proportions (tall thin facades, regular window grid, thin structural posts) — needs squatter, chunkier primary forms like the bedroom furniture.
2. Pillars/lamp posts/awning frames are thin realistic cross-sections; SoftKit language wants oversized capsule/cylinder thickness everywhere, not just on roofs.
3. 鐵窗花 grilles render as flat linework/decal, so they read as a printed sticker rather than a molded 3D toy detail — keep the cultural motif but give it relief/thickness and rounded bar ends.
4. Dog/cat models are faceted low-poly papercraft, clashing with the soft-rounded toon shading elsewhere — biggest cross-asset consistency break.
5. Overall value range is narrow and slightly hazy/desaturated (overcast lighting), killing the glossy-plastic "toy" specular pop — needs punchier saturation and rim/highlight pass on hero shapes.

**Top 3 strengths:**
1. The oversized blobby mushroom/cloud roof canopies are genuinely strong SoftKit shape language — the single best element in the kit.
2. Bedroom interior nails it: rounded furniture corners, round window, plush bear, soft rug — use it as the proportion/scale benchmark for exteriors.
3. Consistent toon outline + pastel palette gives a cohesive base look; the fix is proportion/volume, not palette or line style.
