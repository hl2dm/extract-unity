Score: 6/10

**Reads as real buildings painted pastel?** Yes — the shophouse proportions, window/door spacing, and roofline rhythm closely track realistic Taiwanese street architecture; the pastel palette and black outlines read as a filter over that geometry rather than a redesign from toy-block primitives.

**Top 5 issues**
1. Mass/proportion: storefronts are real-world width/height ratios with thin walls and sharp eaves — a toy diorama needs squashed, chunky, over-scaled forms (fat columns, oversized rounded eaves, shrunk upper floors) to read as "toy," not just rounded corners.
2. 鐵窗花 grilles sit as flat printed/decal circles on the façade plane with no depth, shadow, or inset — reads as a texture swap, undercutting the cultural detail it's meant to showcase. They should be beveled/extruded geometry casting their own AO.
3. The street itself (asphalt, lane markings, crosswalk, utility poles) is rendered in realistic urban-planning layout/scale — a toy-town would compress the road, use toy-scale poles/signage, and curve the street.
4. The dog (r_dog.png) breaks style: naturalistic muzzle shading, thin legs, and realistic proportions clash with the chunky soft-shape language of the buildings/bedroom props.
5. Flat, low-saturation lighting across street scenes (hazy gradient sky, soft shadows) feels like realistic dusk lighting, not the punchy, high-key toy-shelf lighting the target implies.

**Top 3 strengths**
1. Consistent cel/outline shading and pastel palette applied uniformly across street, bedroom, and UI.
2. Bedroom scene nails the look — rounded wardrobe, toy chest, soft rug, chunky bed are genuinely toy-primitive shaped.
3. Color harmony (pink/lavender/mint) is appealing and cohesive brand-wise.
