Yes — reads as real buildings painted pastel: below the mushroom-cap rooflines, the street facades are still realistic shophouse geometry (flat rectangular wall masses, standard window/door counts and proportions, signage, utility boxes, scooters, long soft-AO shadows) with a toon outline and pastel repaint, rather than redesigned toy volumes.

Top 5 issues:
1. Only roofs got the "toy" treatment (big rounded candy caps); the wall bodies beneath stay thin, flat, and real-scale instead of becoming chunky rounded blocks — this is the biggest gap vs. target.
2. Surface noise/detail is too high for a toy diorama: visible wall grain texture, fine signage text, trim lines, cables, AC units read as "shrunk-down real photo" not simplified toy surface.
3. 鐵窗花 window grilles are rendered as thin, photographic-density grids. Keep the motif (good cultural call per the art bible) but thicken the bars into rounded, toy-safe pastel strokes so it reads as a decorative pattern, not literal ironwork.
4. Proportions are naturalistic — door/window sizing, scooter, bus stop are 1:1 real-world scale. Toy language needs exaggeration (oversized rounded trim, squashed windows, bigger handles/knobs), the way the bedroom furniture already does.
5. Lighting/shadows use soft realistic ambient occlusion and long cast shadows on the street; toy sets read better with flatter, brighter, more even lighting and simpler shadow blobs.

Top 3 strengths:
1. Rounded mushroom/bun rooflines give a genuinely strong, distinctive toy silhouette — the best asset to build the rest of the kit around.
2. Bedroom interior nails the target: oversized rounded furniture, pillowy bed, toy chest, bubble decals — this is the benchmark the street should be pulled toward.
3. Cohesive pastel palette + consistent black cel outline across all assets gives a unified toon base.
