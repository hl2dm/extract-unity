Score: 6.5/10

Reads as real buildings painted pastel? **No** — overall silhouettes are toy-ified (bubble roofs, rounded corner buildings, thick ink outlines, cel-shading), so the base language is on-target. But several details (鐵窗花 grilles, shutter patterns, awning stripes, shop signage) are rendered as thin, flat realistic textures rather than chunky toy geometry, which pulls specific facades back toward "recolored real building."

Top 5 issues:
1. 鐵窗花 grilles and shutters are flat painted-on decals, not modeled as thick toy-bar geometry — they read as texture, breaking the diorama illusion up close (r_aunt_ext_0, r_street).
2. Shop awnings/signage (柑仔店, 冰店) use thin realistic striping/typography scale instead of oversized, rounded toy-shop graphics.
3. Scale mismatch: dog, bus, and street props feel small/thin relative to the chunky bubble-roofed buildings — toy dioramas need consistent oversized chunkiness across all props, not just architecture.
4. Bedroom furniture (desk, wardrobe, shelf) has straighter, thinner edges than the exterior buildings — interior doesn't match the exterior's roundness/bevel language, feels like two different art passes.
5. Street/sidewalk surfaces are flat and empty — no chunky toy-brick seams, puddle-gloss, or welt lines that would reinforce "toy set" vs. "painted asphalt."

Top 3 strengths:
1. Strong silhouette language — bubble/dome roofs, rounded window ports (aunt's house portholes) already feel toy-town, not literal Taiwanese architecture traced over.
2. Consistent pastel palette and soft lighting across street, interiors, and bus scenes unifies the world convincingly.
3. Bus and bus stop are the best-executed chunky-toy assets in the set — rounded fenders, oversized wheels, soft bevels — a good reference model for retrofitting the rest.
