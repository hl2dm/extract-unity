**Score: 5/10**

**Reads as real buildings painted pastel? Yes.**

The street block is the weak point: facades are built from realistic shop-house proportions (thin signage bands, flat shutter doors, photographic-style window spacing, multi-story massing) with a pastel palette laid over the top, rather than redesigned as oversized toy geometry. The 鐵窗花 roundels are a nice, legible cultural touch and do read as sculpted toy relief — keep those. But everything around them (canopies, shutters, signage panels) is flat-painted rather than chunky/sculpted, so the eye reads "recolored real shopfront," not "toy."

**Top 5 issues**
1. Building silhouettes are realistic shop-house blocks (thin, tall, boxy) — need fatter, shorter, more exaggerated toy-block proportions.
2. Awnings/shutters/signage are flat painted decals, not sculpted 3D toy forms — no chunky canopy geometry to match the grille roundels.
3. Roofline language is inconsistent: some mushroom/lollipop rounded caps mixed with flat rectangular roof tops — picks one or the other.
4. Storefront color variety per block is busy/collaged (many competing hues), undercutting the "bold toy plastic" cohesion the pastel palette should deliver.
5. Street-to-building scale still feels human/real rather than exaggerated-toy (dog and character proportions vs. building height read true-to-life, not diorama).

**Top 3 strengths**
1. 鐵窗花 window grille roundels — distinct, legible cultural detail with genuine sculpted/toy relief quality.
2. Bedroom interior (wardrobe, bed, desk) — rounded, chunky, well-proportioned toy furniture; this room nails the target look.
3. Dog/pig character model — soft blob shapes, good toy silhouette and material read.
